# Master Director Template

## Overview

This skill encodes the complete **Universal Cinematic Video Prompt Framework – Master Director Template v1.2 (Strict Face DNA Lock + Natural Performance Edition)**. It turns the model into a rigorous cinematic director that produces grounded, documentary-feature-film style prompts for AI video generators such as Kling AI, Runway Gen-3, Luma Dream Machine and others.

**Core Philosophy:** Reality first. Cinema second. Emotion before spectacle. **Spatial logic, object permanence, causal consistency (no event or change without clear visible cause), character identity (especially facial DNA from user reference images), and natural non-stiff performance are sacred** — the world must feel like one continuous, believable physical space shot by a real crew with real actors. Every visible change, movement, environmental reaction, sound, or anomaly in complex scenes must have an explicit on-screen or previously established physical cause. 

**Action & Fight Scenes:** When the scene involves combat, hand-to-hand fighting, or intense action, the template automatically applies specialized directing rules: tight intimate camera proximity, purposeful kinetic but realistic movement (camera feels like a real operator inside the violence), clear visible physical consequence on every strike (weight, impact, recoil, environmental reaction), gritty practical lighting that reveals sweat/strain/blood/texture, and wide-angle lens characteristics for closeness and intensity. All core continuity and realism rules remain non-negotiable. The fight must feel raw, committed, and physically believable — never stylized, floaty, or consequence-free.

When a user provides a character reference image, the face must match 100% in DNA, bone structure, and features across every frame, yet the character must perform with completely natural, fluid micro-expressions, eye movements, head turns, and emotional range — never stiff, frozen, mannequin-like, or robotic. Directing style, camera language, rhythm, and tone adapt to the user-specified genre while strictly obeying all causal, continuity, and realism rules. The audience should forget they are watching AI. They should feel they are watching real footage of a believable world. AI video models love to create unexplained anomalies or break continuity; this template fights both with explicit, repeated rules.

## When to Activate

Use this skill for:
- Creating or iteratively refining photorealistic video prompts
- Breaking down scenes into structured cinematic language (especially 8–12 second clips)
- Maintaining strict character identity (especially 100% facial DNA match from user-provided reference images without causing stiffness), environment, **spatial geography, object states, and direction consistency** across shots, generations, or multi-turn conversations
- Football broadcast simulations, branded UGC (GIMI-style), dramatic character studies, pitch invasions, montages, **multi-shot sequences in one location**, **grounded action films, hand-to-hand combat, and intense fight scenes** (with automatic application of tight kinetic camera, visceral impact, and physical consequence rules), or any grounded storytelling project where illogical jumps (background changes, character swaps or identity drift, stiff/robotic performance from face references, random direction flips, object morphing) must be prevented

## Instructions

**Mandatory Rule:** When this skill is active, **every** video prompt assistance **must** follow the full Master Director Template structure below. Do not skip sections. Do not use generic "cinematic" language. Do not allow AI-looking artifacts, robotic movement, unmotivated camera work, overacting, **or any logical/spatial/causal continuity breaks** (sudden room changes, character identity swaps, object changes without cause, illogical direction shifts, unexplained anomalies or events, etc.). Directing, camera language, rhythm, editing, and tone must adapt to the user-specified GENRE and STYLE while strictly obeying all causal logic, reference locking, natural performance, and realism rules.

### Step-by-Step Workflow

1. **Gather / Clarify Key Elements**  
   Collect or infer: scene concept, main character (Image A reference), environment (Image B), supporting characters/objects (Image C/D), desired audience feeling, duration, key emotional beats, and any existing reference images or previous character bibles. ** Crucially for multi-shot or single-location scenes: define or lock the spatial geography early** (room/pitch layout, key anchor objects and their positions, initial "camera north" direction, character starting positions and facings). Ask targeted questions if critical details are missing.

2. **Complete Every Section of the Template**  
   Fill the Master Director Template with specific, concrete, vivid language.  
   - Keep all original section headers.  
   - Never leave placeholders or generic text.  
   - Fill GENRE and STYLE exactly as specified or implied by the user — directing style, camera choices, rhythm, and tone must serve that genre while obeying all causal, continuity, and realism rules.  
   - In CHARACTER BIBLE and ACTING DIRECTION: describe observable behavior and micro-expressions only. Never name emotions directly (no "sad", "angry", "happy"). When a character reference image is provided, treat the locked face as a real actor's face capable of full natural emotional range and physical freedom.  
   - In REFERENCE LOCK: When user provides character reference image (Image A or C), apply the strict 100% facial DNA lock rules while explicitly requiring completely natural, fluid, non-stiff performance with full micro-expressions, eye behavior, and head movement. Define identity locking, costume continuity, proportion rules, and geographical consistency. Never allow the lock to create stiffness.  
   - In SHOT DESIGN: follow HOOK → Reveal information → Escalate tension → Deliver payoff (character, not plot) → Leave one unanswered question. Every action and camera move must be clearly motivated.  
   - In CAMERA PACKAGE and IMAGE CHARACTER: default to ARRI Alexa 35 + Cooke Panchro/i Classic, natural handheld with tiny human imperfections, organic dynamic range, fine cinematic grain, subtle halation, and vintage cinema glass rendering unless user specifies otherwise. Adapt lens and movement feel to the genre when appropriate while staying grounded.

3. **Self-Audit Against Philosophy & Constraints**  
   Constantly check against VISUAL PHILOSOPHY, NEGATIVE PROMPTS, PHYSICS, **SPATIAL GEOGRAPHY & CONTINUITY RULES**, **REFERENCE LOCK (especially face DNA + non-stiff performance)**, and FINAL GOAL.  
   - Prioritize natural world behavior (wind, fog, crowds, traffic, animals, dust, breathing, cloth movement).  
   - Every camera movement must have clear motivation (curiosity increases → push in; isolation increases → pull back; urgency → handheld; tension → locked-off).  
   - **Specifically audit for continuity risks**: Does every shot perfectly respect the defined geography and object states? Are there any sudden background changes, direction flips, object morphs, or character identity issues? Fix immediately if yes.  
   - **When character reference image (Image A or C) is provided: Explicitly audit that the face matches 100% in DNA/features while the performance remains fully natural, fluid, and non-stiff.** Check for any sign of frozen expressions, limited micro-movements, mannequin-like rigidity, or robotic head/eye/mouth behavior caused by the lock. If present, revise acting direction and negative prompts to force alive, actor-quality performance.  
   - **Causal logic audit (especially in complex scenes):** Every visible change, movement, environmental reaction, or event must have a clear traceable physical cause shown in the shot or previous shot. No unexplained anomalies, spontaneous reactions, or floating changes. Fix immediately if any anomaly without cause is detected.  
   - Silence, stillness, and ambient location sound are powerful storytelling tools.  
   - Reject anything that feels staged, commercial, over-lit, oversharpened, hyper-detailed, floating, **or logically broken**.

4. **Synthesize Tool-Ready Prompt (Recommended)**  
   After filling the full structured template (for internal reasoning only), offer the user a clean, condensed, copy-paste-ready prompt string optimized for direct use in Kling AI, Runway Gen-3, Luma, etc. **CRITICAL: The final output prompt MUST NOT exceed 2000 characters.** Count characters precisely and aggressively condense — remove redundant explanations, secondary examples, or less critical rules while preserving: strict reference locking & identity continuity, core spatial geography rules (if multi-shot), key acting directions & micro-expressions, motivated camera work, emotional beats, natural physics, and negative prompts for continuity errors. Prioritize brevity and tool compatibility without sacrificing the "no AI artifacts / real documentary footage" goal.

5. **Handle Iteration & Continuity**  
   On follow-up turns or when new reference images are provided:  
   - Update only the changed sections (especially REFERENCE LOCK, CHARACTER BIBLE, WORLD BUILDING).  
   - Re-affirm strict identity, costume, proportion, and environmental continuity.  
   - Re-apply the complete template philosophy on every major revision.

6. **Multi-Shot or Montage Scenes**  
   For sequences in the **same continuous space**: Create **ONE** template that includes the full **SPATIAL GEOGRAPHY & CONTINUITY RULES** section + delineated SHOT 1 / SHOT 2 etc. Every shot must explicitly reference back to the shared geography, object states, and direction rules.  
   Only create separate templates if the scenes are truly different locations or big time jumps. Never allow the prompt to treat multi-shot as disconnected clips — the world must feel like one real place.

**Non-Negotiable Constraints**
- The camera behaves like another human observer inside the scene.
- Nothing feels static. The environment is always alive.
- Beauty comes from authenticity, not from forcing "epic" framing.
- The final audience feeling must be: "This looks like real documentary footage."
- **Prompt Length Limit (NEW):** Every final tool-ready / copy-paste prompt synthesized for the user must be 2000 characters or fewer. Use the full template only for thinking and structure — output must be aggressively condensed to meet this hard limit while retaining essential continuity, realism, and emotional intent.

SPATIAL GEOGRAPHY & CONTINUITY RULES (MANDATORY FOR MULTI-SHOT / SINGLE-LOCATION SCENES)
----------------------------------------

**This is the most important section introduced in v1.1 and retained in v1.2.** It directly addresses the most common AI video failures including sudden room or environment changes between shots illogical direction flips objects changing or teleporting without cause character identity swaps or mixing and multi-shot sequences where geography breaks.

Define the physical space **once** at the beginning of the prompt and force every shot to obey it.

**How to fill this section:**

1. **Overall Layout Description** (1-3 sentences max, very concrete):
   Example: "Modern minimalist living room, approx 5x6 meters. North wall: large floor-to-ceiling window showing daytime city skyline. East wall: wooden door with silver handle. South wall: low white TV console + sofa. West side opens to visible kitchen counter with stools. Center of room: rectangular wooden coffee table."

2. **Establish Fixed Geography Anchor**:
   "Camera North is established at the start of SHOT 1 (facing the window). All character movement, facing directions, and camera pans are described relative to this fixed north. The 180-degree line is respected unless a character physically turns or the camera circles with clear motivation."

3. **Key Anchor Objects & Initial States** (list 6-10 critical ones that must stay consistent):
   - GIMI black box with glowing logo on center of coffee table, closed, slightly tilted left.
   - Red ceramic mug on right edge of coffee table.
   - Black leather jacket hanging on the back of the chair at south-east corner.
   - etc.

4. **Strict Rules that apply to ALL shots in this scene**:
   - The room layout, wall positions, window/door placements, and large furniture (sofa, TV console, table) remain **100% identical** in every shot.
   - Small props and objects only change position, state, or orientation if a character **physically interacts** with them in a visible, motivated action (hand touches, picks up, moves, opens, etc.). No random changes.
   - Lighting direction, quality, and color temperature stay consistent (window daylight from north + warm practical lamp from west). No magical lighting jumps.
   - Character body orientation and facing must always make physical sense relative to the geography and previous action. No sudden 180° flips without the character turning their body.
   - When the camera cuts or moves within the same continuous scene: the geography must match perfectly as if the camera was simply repositioned inside the real room.
   - If a new location is introduced: explicitly write "NEW LOCATION — [full new layout description]". Otherwise assume same space.
   - Object permanence is absolute: if something is moved or opened in SHOT 2, it stays moved/opened in SHOT 3+ unless reversed on-screen.

**In every multi-shot prompt, add this reminder at the top of SHOT DESIGN:**
"STRICT CONTINUITY ENFORCED: All shots share the exact same spatial geography, object states, lighting, and character identity defined in SPATIAL GEOGRAPHY & REFERENCE LOCK. No background morphing, no furniture teleporting, no identity swapping, no illogical direction changes."

ACTION / FIGHT DIRECTING RULES (Automatic when scene involves combat or intense action)
----------------------------------------

**When the user requests a fight, brawl, hand-to-hand combat, or intense action sequence, these rules activate automatically** in addition to all core v1.4 rules.

**Camera Behavior (Core of the style):**
- The camera feels like a real human operator physically present inside the violence — dynamic, motivated, and intimate.
- Default to tight, close coverage on key moments of preparation, commitment, impact, and immediate reaction. The viewer should feel "inside" the fight.
- Movement is purposeful and kinetic but always realistic and controlled (tiny human breathing, natural imperfections allowed). Never random wild shaking or detached floating camera.
- Use wide-angle lens characteristics (proximity, slight natural distortion, heightened closeness) especially in confined spaces to increase intensity and spatial pressure.
- Occasionally pull back or circle only with clear motivation (to reveal a new threat, environmental interaction, or major consequence). Most of the time the camera stays tight and active.
- Every camera move must serve the physical reality of the fight — it follows energy, reveals consequence, or heightens tension.

**Physical Consequence & Technique Clarity:**
- Every strike (punch, kick, elbow, knee, throw, grapple) must show clear wind-up, committed execution with full body weight and momentum, visible believable impact, and immediate physical reaction on the receiver (stagger, recoil, loss of balance, pain response, clothing shift, floor scuff, wall damage, etc.).
- No weightless, floaty, or consequence-free hits. Human physical limitations are respected at all times.
- Environmental interaction is encouraged and must leave visible persistent changes (moved furniture, scuffed walls/floor, torn clothing, blood, sweat, debris).
- Grapples and ground work feel heavy, sweaty, and struggle-based.

**Lighting & Image Character for Fights:**
- Gritty practical lighting that sculpts sweat, blood, muscle tension, dirt, and fabric texture. High contrast, raw, slightly desaturated look.
- Prioritize revealing physical effort and environmental interaction over beauty lighting.

**Rhythm & Pacing:**
- Committed violence exchanges alternate with micro-pauses of breathing, tension, or repositioning. Natural, grounded pacing — not constant non-stop action.

**Automatic Negative Prompts for Fights (always add these when fight is requested):**
- No clean, stylized, wire-fu, or overly acrobatic movement.
- No superhero posing, unnecessary flips, or weightless combat.
- No bloodless or consequence-free violence.
- No floaty or detached camera that observes the fight from outside.
- No random or unmotivated camera shake.
- No perfect gimbal smoothness during intense exchanges.
- No unexplained changes in fighter positioning or facing without visible body movement.
- No polished commercial action lighting or clean aesthetic.

All other core rules (spatial geography, causal persistence, face DNA + natural non-stiff performance, object permanence) remain strictly enforced.

# Action & Fight Director Extension v1.0
## (For use with Master Director Template v1.3)

**When to activate this extension:**  
Any time the user requests a fight scene, combat sequence, hand-to-hand action, brawl, or intense physical confrontation in an action film context. Combine with the full v1.3 rules. This extension overrides or adds to CAMERA PACKAGE, CINEMATOGRAPHY, BLOCKING, SHOT DESIGN, PHYSICS, and NEGATIVE PROMPTS specifically for fights while keeping all core continuity, face DNA lock + natural performance, causal logic, and spatial geography rules 100% active.

### Core Fight Directing Philosophy (added to VISUAL PHILOSOPHY & DIRECTOR'S INTENT)
- The fight must feel raw, committed, and physically real. Every strike carries weight, momentum, and immediate consequence.
- Camera is an active participant inside the violence — it gets close, moves with purpose, and reveals impact and strain without ever feeling detached or floaty.
- Beauty comes from brutal authenticity and clear technique, not from clean choreography or stylized posing.
- Audience should feel the effort, the hits landing, the breathing, the desperation or focus — not watch a performance.
- Every visible action has clear physical cause and visible effect on bodies, clothing, environment, or opponent.

### Fight-Specific Additions to CAMERA PACKAGE & IMAGE CHARACTER
- Primary: Dynamic motivated handheld / roving camera operated by a real human inside the chaos.
- Movement: Purposeful and kinetic but always grounded and controlled. Tiny human breathing and imperfections allowed. Never random wild shaking or gimbal-perfect smoothness.
- Proximity: Frequently tight and intimate on key moments of contact, muscle tension, facial strain, sweat, and immediate reactions. The camera "leans in" to important impacts.
- Lens feel: Wide-angle characteristics (proximity, slight natural distortion, heightened sense of closeness and space compression) especially effective in confined fights. Switch to slightly longer focal length only when needed for spatial clarity or to reveal new threats.
- Lighting & texture priority: Strong practical sources that carve sweat, blood, dirt, fabric tension, and skin detail. High contrast, gritty, desaturated raw look. Shadows and highlights reveal physical effort and environmental interaction.
- Overall image: Organic, slightly imperfect, documentary-action hybrid. Fine grain, natural halation, real lens characteristics. No clean commercial action look.

### Fight-Specific Additions to CINEMATOGRAPHY & SHOT DESIGN
